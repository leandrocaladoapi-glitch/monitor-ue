  <title>Failure to respect the rights of individuals: The CNIL fined EXTIA 300 000 EUR</title>
  <link>https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/failure-to-respect-the-rights-of-individuals-the-cnil-fined-extia-300-000-eur_en</link>
  <description>&lt;div class="heading-wrapper"&gt;&lt;h2 id="background-information"&gt;&lt;strong&gt;Background information&lt;/strong&gt;&lt;/h2&gt;&lt;ul&gt;&lt;li&gt;Date of final decision: 21 July 2026&lt;/li&gt;&lt;li&gt;National case&lt;/li&gt;&lt;li&gt;Legal Reference(s): Article 12 (Transparent information, communication and modalities for the exercise of the rights of the data subject), Article 17 (Right to erasure ‘right to be forgotten’)&lt;/li&gt;&lt;li&gt;Decision: Administrative fine&lt;/li&gt;&lt;li&gt;Key words: Data subject rights&lt;/li&gt;&lt;/ul&gt;&lt;/div&gt;&lt;div class="heading-wrapper"&gt;&lt;h2 id="summary-of-the-decision"&gt;&lt;strong&gt;Summary of the Decision&lt;/strong&gt;&lt;/h2&gt;&lt;h3&gt;&lt;strong&gt;Origin of the case &amp;nbsp;&lt;/strong&gt;&lt;/h3&gt;&lt;p&gt;EXTIA, which specialises in IT and engineering, recruits consultants for various technical projects from its client companies.&amp;nbsp;&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;In 2024, the French Data Protection Authority (CNIL) received several complaints from former employees or candidates, relating to difficulties encountered in exercising their right to erasure or ‘right to be forgotten’. With a view to investigating these complaints, and also in the context of the Coordinated Enforcement Framework action on the ‘Right to erasure’ launched on the initiative of the European Data Protection Board in 2025, an audit of EXTIA was carried out in April 2025. It identified breaches of several obligations under the GDPR regarding transparency and respect for individuals’ rights.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;Of the 265 requests for erasure received by the company in 2024, the majority of which came from candidates and, occasionally, former employees, more than three quarters had not been dealt with or had not been dealt with satisfactorily.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;h3&gt;&lt;strong&gt;Key Findings&lt;/strong&gt;&lt;/h3&gt;&lt;p&gt;&lt;strong&gt;Failure to process erasure requests (Articles 12 and 17 GDPR)&lt;/strong&gt;&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;The CNIL’s restricted committee – the body responsible for issuing sanctions – &amp;nbsp;noted that 12 requests for erasure received by the company in 2024 had not been processed. It considered that that failure had adversely affected the rights of those persons, including the right to retain control over their data.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;&lt;strong&gt;Failure to inform individuals of the action taken on their request for erasure (Article 12 GDPR)&lt;/strong&gt;&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;The CNIL’s restricted committee considered that the company had failed to fulfil its obligation to inform the persons who had requested the erasure of their data. It noted that 166 persons who had made a request for erasure in 2024 had not been informed of the action taken on that request. Another 27 people had received this information late (outside the legal one-month deadline), with delays of up to several months.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;h3&gt;&lt;strong&gt;Decision&lt;/strong&gt;&lt;/h3&gt;&lt;p&gt;Consequently, the restricted committee imposed a fine of 300 000 EUR on EXTIA, taking into account the infringement of essential principles relating to the rights of individuals, the number of persons concerned and the fact that EXTIA had already been reminded of its obligations on two occasions.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;&lt;strong&gt;For further information:&lt;/strong&gt;&amp;nbsp;&lt;/p&gt;&lt;ul&gt;&lt;li&gt;&lt;a href="https://www.cnil.fr/en/sanction-failure-rights-individuals-extia"&gt;Failure to respect the rights of individuals: EUR 300,000 fine against EXTIA&lt;/a&gt;&lt;/li&gt;&lt;li&gt;&lt;a href="https://www.cnil.fr/fr/sanction-non-respect-droits-personnes-extia"&gt;Non-respect des droits des personnes : sanction de 300 000 euros à l’encontre de la société EXTIA&lt;/a&gt;&lt;/li&gt;&lt;/ul&gt;&lt;/div&gt;</description>

  <title>Health data breach: the CNIL fined Hôpital Privé de la Loire 500 000 EUR</title>
  <link>https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/health-data-breach-the-cnil-fined-hopital-prive-de-la-loire-500-000-eur_en</link>
  <description>&lt;div class="heading-wrapper"&gt;&lt;h2 id="background-information"&gt;&lt;strong&gt;Background information&lt;/strong&gt;&lt;/h2&gt;&lt;ul&gt;&lt;li&gt;Date of final decision: 3 September 2026&lt;/li&gt;&lt;li&gt;National case&lt;/li&gt;&lt;li&gt;Controller: Hôpital Privé de la Loire&lt;/li&gt;&lt;li&gt;Legal Reference: Article 32 (Security of processing), Article 34 (Communication of a personal data breach to the data subject),&amp;nbsp;&lt;/li&gt;&lt;li&gt;Decision:&amp;nbsp;Administrative fine&lt;/li&gt;&lt;li&gt;Keywords: Cybersecurity, Personal data breaches, Health and research&lt;/li&gt;&lt;/ul&gt;&lt;/div&gt;&lt;div class="heading-wrapper"&gt;&lt;h2 id="summary-of-the-decision"&gt;&lt;strong&gt;Summary of the Decision&lt;/strong&gt;&lt;/h2&gt;&lt;h3&gt;&lt;strong&gt;Origin of the case&lt;/strong&gt;&lt;/h3&gt;&lt;p&gt;In summer 2025, an attacker managed to connect to the Computerised Patient Summary (DPI) of the Hôpital Privé de la Loire (Loire’s private hospital), which centralises all the data of the individuals under care. It thus accessed the data of 524 867 patients (some of them health data) and 202 246 persons designated as “trusted third parties”. As a result of this data breach, the French Data Protection Authority (CNIL) carried out a check that identified several failures of the Hôpital Privé de la Loire to comply with the obligations laid down in the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR).&lt;/p&gt;&lt;h3&gt;&lt;strong&gt;Key findings&lt;/strong&gt;&lt;/h3&gt;&lt;ul&gt;&lt;li&gt;&lt;strong&gt;Failure to ensure the security of personal data (Article 32 GDPR)&lt;/strong&gt;&lt;br&gt;The authentication procedure to connect to the hospital’s e-Health Patient Summary, used by users outside the hospital, in particular doctors not affiliated with the hospital, was not sufficiently robust, due to the lack of VPNs and multifactor authentication means. The attacker took advantage of this vulnerability to access the data. Moreover, the access control policy was inadequate: it did not take account of the concept of care team, so that only professionals actually involved in the care of a patient had access to the information covered by medical confidentiality. This lack of access limitation allowed the attacker, using the credentials of a single user account, to access the data of all hospital patients. Finally, the hospital had not taken measures to detect suspicious activity within the e-Health Patient Summary in real time or in the very short term, and to trigger an alert mechanism if necessary. In those circumstances, the attacker was able to explore the hospital’s e-Health Patient Summary for several days and extract a very large volume of data, without that abnormal activity being detected. This vulnerability has contributed to exacerbating the scale of the data breach.&lt;/li&gt;&lt;li&gt;&lt;strong&gt;Failure to inform data subjects about the data breach (Article 34 GDPR)&lt;/strong&gt;&lt;br&gt;Finally, the restricted committee found that only the patients of the Hôpital Privé de la Loire concerned by the data breach had been informed, but that no direct information had been provided to the 202 246 individuals designated by patients as trusted third parties, even though their personal data had also been stolen by the attacker.&lt;/li&gt;&lt;/ul&gt;&lt;h3&gt;&lt;strong&gt;Decision&lt;/strong&gt;&lt;/h3&gt;&lt;p&gt;The restricted committee – the body of the CNIL responsible for issuing sanctions – imposed a fine of 500 000 EUR on the Hôpital Privé de la Loire, taking into account, inter alia, the lack of awareness of essential security principles, the number of persons concerned, the nature of the data compromised and its financial capacities.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;&lt;strong&gt;Further information:&lt;/strong&gt;&lt;/p&gt;&lt;ul&gt;&lt;li&gt;&lt;a href="https://www.cnil.fr/fr/sanction-hopital-prive-loire"&gt;Violation de données en matière de santé : sanction de 500 000 euros à l’encontre de l’HÔPITAL PRIVÉ DE LA LOIRE&lt;/a&gt;&lt;/li&gt;&lt;li&gt;&lt;a href="https://www.cnil.fr/en/sanction-fine-hopital-prive-loire"&gt;Health data breach: EUR 500,000 fine against HÔPITAL PRIVÉ DE LA LOIRE&lt;/a&gt;&lt;/li&gt;&lt;/ul&gt;&lt;/div&gt;</description>

  <title>EDPB calls for legal basis for cross-regulatory information sharing</title>
  <link>https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/edpb-calls-for-legal-basis-for-cross-regulatory-information-sharing_en</link>
  <description>&lt;p&gt;Dublin, 17 July– At a high-level meeting in Dublin on 16 and 17 July 2026, the European Data Protection Board (EDPB) called for a clear legal basis for the sharing of information among regulators with different competences. The Board also discussed how to further expand efforts to support a consistent application of the General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR), including through more intense cooperation between Data Protection Authorities (DPAs).&lt;/p&gt;&lt;h3&gt;A clear legal basis for efficient cross-regulatory cooperation&lt;/h3&gt;&lt;p&gt;The Board underlines the growing need in the current regulatory environment for effective cooperation between regulators operating in adjacent areas of competence under EU law.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;The EDPB calls upon the European Commission to propose a&lt;strong&gt; legal basis for cross-regulatory information sharing&lt;/strong&gt;. This should enable regulators to exchange information, including confidential information, relevant to enforcement within their respective areas of competence.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;blockquote&gt;&lt;p&gt;First-hand experience of cooperating with other EU digital regulators at both national and EU level has highlighted the need for stronger legislation to facilitate more effective cross-regulatory cooperation, including enhanced information sharing. This will help remove barriers to cooperation and help improve enforcement outcomes and cross-regulatory coherence.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;&lt;strong&gt;EDPB Chair, Anu Talus&lt;/strong&gt;&lt;/p&gt;&lt;/blockquote&gt;&lt;h3&gt;Strengthening consistency and boosting efficient enforcement&lt;/h3&gt;&lt;p&gt;The EDPB recalls the significant progress on cross-border enforcement, as also mentioned in the &lt;a href="https://commission.europa.eu/document/be38d4d5-9a69-4e07-b1fd-59849e9c1edb_en"&gt;second European Commission report on the GDPR&lt;/a&gt;.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;At the same time, DPAs are facing a considerable rise in the number and complexity of complaints, among others as a result of the increased use of AI. This is placing additional strain on already stretched resources limiting all DPAs’ ability to perform all their tasks and exercise their powers under the GDPR efficiently.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;DPAs underlined the need to find practical and, where necessary, legislative solutions to address these challenges, to ensure the efficient enforcement of the GDPR, especially in cases where a large number of people is affected.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;To further boost cross-border enforcement, the Board discussed practical solutions and how to pool resources to further deepen cooperation between DPAs, including the possibility for complaint-receiving authorities to make resources available to lead supervisory authorities, where useful. In addition, the DPAs will organise a series of workshops on enforcement procedures and to exchange information on national practices, also in the context of implementing the upcoming Procedural Regulation.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;blockquote&gt;&lt;p&gt;Greater use of joint operations will help DPAs pool resources and carry out their enforcement actions with greater efficiency. Through information sharing and joint operations, DPAs will support consistent and effective enforcement, better protect individuals and provide greater regulatory clarity and certainty for industry. In addition, in the context of the upcoming Procedural Regulation, which codifies many DPAs’ existing practices, exchanges of information on national practices will be crucial.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;&lt;strong&gt;Des Hogan, Chairperson and Commissioner for Data Protection, Ireland&lt;/strong&gt;&lt;/p&gt;&lt;/blockquote&gt;&lt;p&gt;The Board noted that consistency depends on a broad range of actors and actions, going sometimes beyond the reach of the EDPB, for example when related to national legislation and case law. The EDPB members therefore committed to expanding its dialogue with other actors across the data protection ecosystem, in order to support greater consistency of the GDPR’s application.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;Finally, the EDPB also took stock of the actions taken following the 2025 Helsinki &lt;a href="https://www.edpb.europa.eu/our-work-tools/our-documents/statements/helsinki-statement-enhanced-clarity-support-and-engagement_en" target="_blank"&gt;Statement on enhanced clarity, support and engagement&lt;/a&gt;, from improved speed and quality of its guidance, the adoption of cross-regulatory guidance to making available templates and strengthening stakeholder engagement. The EDPB committed to continue to build on it.&lt;/p&gt;</description>

  <description>&lt;p&gt;&lt;span&gt;Brussels, 14 July–The EDPB has published its&amp;nbsp;&lt;/span&gt;&lt;a href="https://www.edpb.europa.eu/documents/edpb-binding-decisions/binding-decision-12026-on-the-dispute-submitted-by-the-belgian-sa_en"&gt;&lt;span&gt;&lt;u&gt;binding decision of 28 May 2026&amp;nbsp;under Art.65(1)(a) GDPR*.&lt;/u&gt;&lt;/span&gt;&lt;/a&gt;&lt;span&gt;&lt;strong&gt;&lt;u&gt;&amp;nbsp;&lt;/u&gt;&lt;/strong&gt;The decision concerns a&lt;strong&gt;&amp;nbsp;&lt;/strong&gt;dispute submitted by the Belgian Data Protection Authority (DPA) about a complaint against &lt;/span&gt;&lt;em&gt;&lt;span&gt;Vlaamse Radio-en Televisieomroeporganisatie (VRT)&lt;/span&gt;&lt;/em&gt;&lt;span&gt; – a public broadcasting company based in Belgium.&lt;/span&gt;&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;&lt;span&gt;The complaint was lodged with the Austrian DPA by the Austrian-based NGO &lt;/span&gt;&lt;em&gt;&lt;span&gt;Noyb&lt;/span&gt;&lt;/em&gt;&lt;span&gt; on behalf of an individual. It concerns the use of cookie banners on the website of &lt;/span&gt;&lt;em&gt;&lt;span&gt;VRT&lt;/span&gt;&lt;/em&gt;&lt;span&gt;.&lt;/span&gt;&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;&lt;span&gt;The Belgian DPA, acting as Lead Supervisory Authority (LSA), submitted a draft decision proposing to dismiss the complaint on the basis of an alleged abuse of Art.77 GDPR and Art. 80(1) GDPR.   <title>EDPB sheds light on anonymisation and web scraping for generative AI and adopts final version of guidelines on blockchain</title>
  <link>https://www.edpb.europa.eu/news/edpb-sheds-light-on-anonymisation-and-web-scraping-for-generative-ai-and-adopts-final-version_en</link>
  <description>&lt;p&gt;Brussels, 8 July– During its latest plenary, the EDPB has adopted &lt;a href="https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-07/edpb_guidelines_202602_anonymisation_v1_en_0.pdf"&gt;&lt;strong&gt;guidelines on anonymisation&lt;/strong&gt;&lt;/a&gt;&lt;strong&gt; &lt;/strong&gt;and &lt;a href="https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-07/edpb_guidelines_2020603_webscraping_v1_en_0.pdf"&gt;&lt;strong&gt;guidelines on web scraping in the context of generative AI.&lt;/strong&gt;&lt;/a&gt; In addition, the Board has adopted the final version of its &lt;a href="https://www.edpb.europa.eu/system/files/2026-07/edpb_guidelines_202502_blockchain_v2_en.pdf"&gt;&lt;strong&gt;guidelines on the processing of personal data through blockchain technologies.&lt;/strong&gt;&lt;/a&gt;&lt;/p&gt;&lt;h3&gt;Understanding anonymous data&lt;/h3&gt;&lt;p&gt;The new EDPB guidelines &lt;strong&gt;bring clarity to the notion of anonymous data&lt;/strong&gt;, taking also into account the ruling of the Court of Justice of the EU in the case &lt;a href="https://infocuria.curia.europa.eu/tabs/document?source=document&amp;amp;docid=303863&amp;amp;doclang=EN"&gt;C-413/23 P EDPS v SRB of 4 September 2025&lt;/a&gt; and other CJEU jurisprudence.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;blockquote&gt;&lt;p&gt;The guidelines mark a significant milestone in clarifying the notion of anonymous data, establishing clear standards that facilitate the use of data while protecting individuals' fundamental rights.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;In developing these guidelines, we incorporated valuable input from our stakeholder event, showing, once more, our strong commitment to collaborative dialogue as outlined in the EDPB Helsinki statement.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;&lt;strong&gt;EDPB Chair, Anu Talus&lt;/strong&gt;&lt;/p&gt;&lt;/blockquote&gt;&lt;p&gt;Data is anonymous if it does not relate to an identified or identifiable natural person. Whether this is the case may vary from one entity to another.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;Information can &lt;strong&gt;relate&lt;/strong&gt; to an individual because of its &lt;strong&gt;content, purpose, or effect&lt;/strong&gt;. The existence of such a link may not be immediately obvious and could require further analysis.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;An individual is considered &lt;strong&gt;'identified or identifiable'&lt;/strong&gt; if they &lt;strong&gt;can be distinguished from others in a specific context&lt;/strong&gt; using means reasonably likely to be used in a way that makes it possible to treat them differently. Whether the &lt;strong&gt;means are reasonably likely to be used&lt;/strong&gt; will depend on the relevant entity’s perspective and should be assessed in light of all objective factors.&lt;/p&gt;&lt;p&gt;The guidelines also provide a practical framework for organisations to&lt;strong&gt; determine if anonymisation is successful&lt;/strong&gt;.
